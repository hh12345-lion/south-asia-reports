Country information is not always uniform. Different sources may describe the same issue in different ways, particularly where circumstances vary by location, community, period or source.

For solicitors preparing an asylum or immigration case involving South Asia, this can raise an important evidential question: how should conflicting country information be considered within a country expert report?

A country expert may need to examine different sources in context rather than treating one source as automatically determinative. Relevant factors can include the date of the material, its origin, geographical scope, methodology and relevance to the particular issue under consideration.

This article looks at some of the factors that may be relevant when a South Asian country expert encounters differing or apparently conflicting sources.

## Why Country Information May Differ

Differences between sources do not necessarily mean that one source is inaccurate. Sources may examine different aspects of the same issue or cover different periods, locations or groups.

### Differences in Date

Country conditions can change over time. A report, news article, government publication or research document may describe circumstances at a particular point in time.

Information concerning political activity, treatment of a particular community, security conditions or the operation of local institutions may therefore need to be considered in its historical context.

An expert report may distinguish between:

- Information concerning the period relevant to the appellant's account
- Later developments that may provide context
- Older information that may have historical relevance but limited application to current conditions

The date of an important source can therefore affect how its contents are understood.

### Differences in Geographic Scope

Country information may also differ because sources examine different geographical areas.

National-level material may not address conditions in a particular district or city in detail. Equally, information about one locality may not necessarily represent conditions elsewhere in the country.

Where a case involves a particular location, the expert may consider whether available country information adequately addresses that area and whether more geographically specific material is relevant.

### Differences in Subject Matter

Sources may appear to conflict when they are actually addressing different questions.

For example, one source may discuss legal protections available to a particular group, while another may consider how those protections operate in practice.

Similarly, one source may describe formal government policy while another reports experiences of individuals or communities.

Identifying what each source actually addresses can help distinguish a genuine conflict from a difference in scope or subject matter.

## Assessing the Sources in Context

Where country information differs, an expert may consider several characteristics of the material before forming an opinion.

### Source Origin

The origin of information may be relevant to understanding its evidential value.

Potential sources can include government publications, international organisations, academic research, established human rights organisations, reputable media reporting, local publications and other publicly available material.

The category of a source does not, by itself, determine whether it is reliable or relevant. The expert may also consider who produced the material, what information was available to the author and the purpose for which it was produced.

### Methodology

The way information was obtained may also affect how it should be interpreted.

For example, a research report based on interviews may provide a different type of information from an official statistical publication. A media report may describe an individual incident, while a broader research study may examine developments over a longer period.

These differences do not necessarily make one type of source preferable in every situation. An expert may instead consider what each source can reasonably establish in relation to the question being addressed.

### Date and Currency

The age of a source can be relevant where circumstances are changing.

Older material may still provide useful historical context, but it may not describe conditions at the time relevant to the case.

More recent information may describe developments that occurred after the events relied upon by an appellant.

A country expert report may therefore distinguish between historical country conditions and information concerning more recent developments.

## Dealing With Apparently Conflicting Evidence

Where relevant sources provide different information, the expert may examine why the difference exists rather than simply selecting one account.

### Compare the Propositions Being Made

The precise proposition supported by each source can be important.

For example, one source may report that a particular problem has occurred, while another may indicate that the problem is less widespread than some reports suggest.

Those statements may not necessarily be mutually exclusive.

An expert may therefore consider distinctions involving:

- The existence of a reported problem
- Its reported frequency or prevalence
- Its geographical distribution
- The groups or individuals affected
- The period during which it was reported
- The circumstances in which it may arise

This approach can help avoid treating different types of information as direct contradictions when they may address different aspects of the same issue.

### Consider the Population Covered

Sources may concern different groups of people.

For example, information about political activists may have limited application to people who are politically inactive. Material concerning one religious or ethnic community may also have limited relevance to another community.

Where an asylum claim concerns a particular profile, an expert may consider whether the available source material relates sufficiently closely to that profile.

### Consider Local Factors

Country information may address national, regional or local circumstances differently.

A source discussing national conditions may not answer a question about a particular district, city or community. Where local circumstances are material to the case, the expert may consider whether more specific information is available and how much weight can reasonably be placed on it.

This does not mean that local information should automatically be preferred. Its relevance, provenance and limitations may also require consideration.

## How an Expert Report May Explain Differing Sources

A country expert report can explain the basis of the expert's assessment where relevant sources provide different information.

### Identify the Relevant Question

The report may first identify the particular country issue requiring expert consideration.

This could concern treatment of a particular group, the availability of state protection, local conditions, political activity or another country-specific issue.

A clearly defined question can help keep the analysis focused on the matters for which specialist country knowledge has been requested.

### Set Out the Relevant Evidence

The expert may identify the sources that are relevant to the question.

Where material points in different directions, the report can acknowledge the difference rather than presenting only information that supports one interpretation.

The expert can then explain how the relevant material has been considered and, where appropriate, the basis for any professional opinion reached.

### Explain Limitations

Country information is not always complete.

There may be limited reporting from particular regions or communities. Some events may receive substantial public attention while others receive relatively little reporting.

An expert may therefore identify gaps or limitations in the available material.

Where the available evidence does not allow a firm conclusion, the report can make that limitation clear rather than presenting uncertainty as certainty.

## Information Solicitors May Provide When Instructing an Expert

The focus of an expert assessment may depend partly on the information supplied with the instruction.

Where relevant, solicitors may wish to provide information that helps define the particular country question requiring specialist consideration.

This may include:

- The appellant's country and relevant locality
- The nature of the claimed risk
- Relevant dates and events
- The particular group, organisation or community involved
- The specific questions on which expert evidence is sought
- Country material that appears to provide differing information

Focused instructions can help the expert understand which aspects of the available country evidence are material to the case.

## When Conflicting Sources Cannot Be Fully Reconciled

Not every difference between sources can necessarily be resolved.

There may be circumstances where relevant sources provide different accounts and the available material does not establish a clear explanation.

In such circumstances, an expert report may distinguish between what the available evidence indicates and what cannot reasonably be established from that evidence.

Expert evidence is based on the information available to the expert and the expert's professional knowledge. It does not necessarily remove uncertainty where the underlying country information is itself incomplete, inconsistent or developing.

## Distinguishing Evidence From Expert Opinion

It can also be useful to distinguish between the information contained in a source and the opinion expressed by the expert.

A source may report that a particular event occurred or that a particular condition has been documented. The expert may then consider whether that information is relevant to the specific country question and explain what, if anything, it may indicate.

This distinction can help keep the report transparent about:

- What the underlying source says
- What the expert considers the material to indicate
- Any limitations affecting the assessment
- Areas where the available evidence remains uncertain

The purpose is not necessarily to eliminate every difference between sources, but to explain how relevant material has been considered in relation to the question requiring expert evidence.

## Conclusion

Conflicting country information can arise in asylum and immigration cases involving South Asian countries. Differences may reflect variations in date, location, population, methodology or the particular issue examined.

A South Asian country expert may consider these factors when assessing differing sources and explaining their relevance to a particular case. Where sources cannot be fully reconciled, identifying the uncertainty and limitations may be more appropriate than presenting the available material as conclusive.

For solicitors, clearly identifying the country-specific question and providing relevant background information can help an expert focus the assessment on the issues for which specialist country knowledge is required.
